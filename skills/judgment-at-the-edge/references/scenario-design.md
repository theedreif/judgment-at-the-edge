# Scenario Design for Judgment at the Edge

## Start with the decision

Do not begin by writing a story. Define the judgment point first.

Specify:
- actor;
- mission;
- operating environment;
- consequential decision;
- information available;
- uncertainty;
- relevant limits;
- evidence to observe.

## Build the signal

Create a cue that requires interpretation rather than merely recall.

Useful signals may be incomplete, competing, delayed, ambiguous, or inconsistent when that reflects the real operating environment.

## Establish conditions

Give enough context for reasoning while avoiding irrelevant detail. The scenario should force the participant to interpret what is true now.

## Make limits consequential

Authority, policy, safety, time, technical capability, resources, and mission requirements should affect the available choices.

Never invent real-world thresholds or policies. Mark fictional scenario constraints clearly or obtain authoritative sources.

## Require a decision

Avoid scenarios where the participant can succeed by simply describing the problem. Require a choice, action, escalation, intervention, or stop decision.

## Capture evidence

Observe more than the final answer. Capture:
- what the participant noticed;
- information they sought;
- assumptions they made;
- limits they recognized;
- timing;
- choice;
- rationale;
- communication;
- adaptation when conditions changed.

## Add purposeful friction

Friction should reveal capability, not create arbitrary difficulty.

Examples:
- a second signal;
- a degraded sensor;
- conflicting information;
- time compression;
- resource loss;
- an automation surprise;
- a handoff;
- an authority boundary;
- a changed mission condition.

## Debrief

Use:

**What did you notice? → What did you think was true? → What constrained you? → Why did you act? → What evidence supports that? → What would make you change your decision?**
