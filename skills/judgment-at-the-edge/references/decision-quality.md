# Decision Quality Under Uncertainty

## Decision quality is not outcome quality

Outcomes contain factors outside the decision-maker's control. Review the decision using the information available at the time.

Ask:

1. What information was actually available?
2. What uncertainty was visible?
3. Which assumptions were made?
4. Which constraints applied?
5. Were plausible alternatives considered?
6. Was the action proportionate to the evidence and stakes?
7. What new information would have changed the choice?
8. Was escalation or intervention appropriate?
9. What should be updated after the result?

## Avoid hindsight bias

Do not rewrite the decision moment using information learned later.

When conducting an after-action review, separate:

**THEN** — what was known, believed, constrained, and possible at the moment.

**NOW** — what the outcome and later evidence reveal.

The learning occurs in the difference.

## Defensibility

A defensible decision does not mean an infallible decision. It means the reasoning can be reconstructed and examined against the evidence, uncertainty, and limits that existed at the time.

## Update rule

Convert lessons into something operational:
- a changed threshold;
- a revised scenario;
- a clearer escalation trigger;
- a field cue;
- a new evidence requirement;
- a corrected assumption;
- a question requiring authoritative resolution.
