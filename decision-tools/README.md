# Decision Field Card v1.0

### A compact decision architecture for the moment the plan stops matching reality.

> **The tool must reduce cognitive load without removing judgment.**

The Decision Field Card turns the **Judgment at the Edge** framework into a practical sequence that can be used during scenario design, training, operations, coaching, or after-action review.

## The Card

| Step | Field Question | Record |
|---|---|---|
| **SIGNAL** | What changed? | What cue, anomaly, event, or information deserves attention? |
| **CONDITION** | What is true now? | What does the operating environment actually look like? |
| **LIMIT** | What constrains me? | Time, authority, policy, safety, system capability, resources, uncertainty? |
| **DECISION** | What action is justified? | What will I do given the signal, condition, and limits? |
| **EVIDENCE** | What makes this defensible? | What did I observe, why did I act, and what happened next? |

> # SIGNAL → CONDITION → LIMIT → DECISION → EVIDENCE

## Before · During · After

### BEFORE
**What would make me change my mind?**

Identify disconfirming evidence before commitment makes it harder to see.

### DURING
**What signal would make me intervene, escalate, stop, or change course?**

Define the boundary before pressure arrives.

### AFTER
**What happened—and what should update?**

Separate the quality of the decision from the quality of the outcome.

> **Do not ask only: “Was I right?”**  
> Ask: **“Was this a good decision given what I knew at the time?”**

## How to Use It

**For operators:** use the five questions to structure a consequential decision without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

**For instructors:** build scenarios that force learners to identify signals, interpret conditions, recognize limits, choose an action, and defend it with evidence.

**For observers:** capture the decision process rather than scoring only the final outcome.

**For after-action review:** compare what was known at decision time with what became known afterward.

**For human-machine teams:** explicitly identify the signal or boundary that should cause a human to question, intervene, escalate, or stop automation.

## Design Principle

The card is deliberately small.

A field tool that requires ideal conditions, lengthy explanation, or extensive documentation has failed its operating environment.

> **If it cannot survive the field, it does not count.**
